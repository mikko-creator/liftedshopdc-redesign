How Long Do Edibles Take to Hit?

ORDER DELIVERY(771) 216-1684

Usually 30 to 90 minutes, though the outer edges run from about 20 minutes to well over 2 hours.

The delay exists because an edible has to travel a longer road than smoke does. When you inhale, THC crosses from your lungs into your bloodstream in seconds. When you eat, it has to pass through your stomach, get absorbed in your small intestine, and then run through your liver before it reaches circulation.

A few things speed it up or slow it down:

  • Product type matters most: Gummies, chocolates, and baked goods all go through full digestion. Lozenges, mints, and tinctures held under the tongue partly absorb through the tissue in your mouth, which can bring onset down to 15 to 30 minutes. Drinks made with nano-emulsified THC are often faster still.
  • Whether you’ve eaten: On an empty stomach, onset tends to be faster but less predictable. Taken alongside a meal with some fat in it, onset is slower, but absorption is often more complete, which can mean a stronger overall experience.
  • Your metabolism: This one you can’t control, and it accounts for a lot of the person-to-person variation.

Why Edibles Feel Different From Smoking

This is the part worth understanding, because it explains nearly everything else about edibles.

When THC passes through your liver, an enzyme converts a significant portion of it into a compound called 11-hydroxy-THC. That compound crosses into the brain more readily than the delta-9-THC you get from smoking, and it’s generally considered more potent.

So an edible isn’t just a slower version of a joint. It’s a chemically different experience: longer, often more physical, and frequently more intense at the same nominal milligram count.

It’s also why tolerance doesn’t transfer cleanly. Someone who smokes daily can still be caught out by a 20mg gummy.

How Long Does an Edible High Last?

The main effects generally run 4 to 8 hours from onset, with the peak sitting in the 2 to 4 hour window.

By comparison, a smoked high usually lasts 1 to 3 hours. We cover that in more detail in how long does a weed high last.

What extends it:

  • Dose. The most significant factor by a wide margin. 5mg might give you three or four hours. 50mg can run past twelve.
  • Body composition. THC is fat-soluble, so it stores in fatty tissue and releases gradually.
  • Tolerance. Regular consumers often report shorter, less intense effects at the same dose.
  • Sleep. Many people find that sleeping through the tail end shortens the perceived duration considerably.

One practical note: impairment outlasts the obvious high. Judgment, reaction time, and coordination can stay affected after you feel more or less normal. Don’t drive, and give yourself more margin than you think you need.

How Long Do Edibles Stay in Your System?

This is a different question from how long the effects last, and the answer is much longer.

The high is over in hours. THC metabolites can linger for days or weeks because they accumulate in fat tissue and clear slowly.

Rough detection windows:

Test type Occasional use Frequent use Daily use
Urine 3 to 7 days 10 to 15 days 30+ days
Saliva 24 to 72 hours Up to a week Up to a week
Blood 1 to 2 days Up to a week Up to a week
Hair Up to 90 days Up to 90 days Up to 90 days

Treat these as broad ranges. Dose, frequency, body fat percentage, metabolism, and the specific test’s sensitivity all shift the numbers, sometimes substantially.

Dosing: How Much Is Too Much?

Milligrams of THC per serving, which should be printed on any properly labeled product:

Dose Typical effect
1 to 2.5mg Very mild. Minimal impairment. A common microdose range.
2.5 to 5mg Light. A sensible starting point if you’re new to edibles.
5 to 10mg Standard single serving for most people.
10 to 20mg Strong. Noticeable impairment likely.
20mg+ Experienced consumers with established tolerance.

The rule that prevents almost every bad edible experience: wait a full two hours before taking more.

The delay is the trap. You eat a gummy, feel nothing after 45 minutes, decide it was weak, eat another, and then both arrive at once around the 90-minute mark. Nearly every story that begins “I had a terrible time with edibles” has this shape.

If you’re new, start at 2.5mg or split a 5mg serving in half. You can always take more tomorrow. You can’t take less once it’s eaten.

Worth checking with a doctor first if you take prescription medication, since THC can interact with blood thinners and sedatives among others.

What to Do If You’ve Taken Too Much

Uncomfortable, sometimes genuinely unpleasant, but not dangerous in the way people fear. There are no recorded deaths from THC overconsumption alone. It will pass.

What helps:

  • Get somewhere calm and familiar. Dim the lights, put on something undemanding, reduce input.
  • Drink water and eat something. Both help, and low blood sugar makes anxiety worse.
  • Remind yourself of the timeline. Knowing the peak is at hours 2 to 4, and it tapers from there, makes the experience much more manageable.
  • Sleep if you can. The most effective option available.
  • Try CBD if you have it. Some people find it takes the edge off, though the evidence is mixed.

When to seek medical help: severe chest pain, a racing heart that won’t settle, persistent vomiting, or confusion severe enough that you can’t orient yourself. These are uncommon, but if you’re worried, call. Nobody will be annoyed with you for asking.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do edibles take to kick in?

Usually 30 to 90 minutes. Lozenges, mints, and tinctures held under the tongue can work in 15 to 30 minutes because some absorption happens in the mouth. Gummies, chocolates, and baked goods take longer because they go through full digestion.

How long does an edible high last?

Typically 4 to 8 hours, with the peak between 2 and 4 hours. Larger doses can push total duration past 12 hours, and some people notice mild residual grogginess the next morning.

Why haven't my edibles kicked in yet?

Most likely you haven’t waited long enough, since 90 minutes is well within normal. A large meal beforehand can also delay onset. Occasionally a product is underdosed or mislabeled. Either way, wait a full two hours before taking more.

How long do edibles stay in your system?

Far longer than the effects last. Urine tests commonly detect THC metabolites for 3 to 7 days after occasional use and 30 days or more with daily use. Saliva and blood windows are shorter, and hair testing can reach back 90 days.

Do edibles hit harder than smoking?

Often, yes. Your liver converts a portion of the THC into 11-hydroxy-THC, which is generally considered more potent and enters the brain more readily. This is why smoking tolerance doesn’t reliably carry over to edibles.

How long should I wait before taking another edible?

At least two hours. Taking a second dose before the first has fully arrived is the single most common reason people have a bad time with edibles.

Does eating food make edibles work faster?

Taking an edible with fatty food generally slows onset but can improve absorption, meaning a stronger and longer experience. An empty stomach tends to be faster but less predictable.

Can you sleep off an edible?

Yes, and for many people it’s the most comfortable way to handle a dose that’s stronger than intended. You may wake up a little foggy, but the effects continue to clear while you sleep.

Getting the Dose Right Is Most of the Battle

Edibles are one of the most pleasant ways to consume cannabis when the dose and timing are right, and one of the least pleasant when they aren’t. The difference is almost entirely patience.

Start low, wait two hours, and take notes on what worked. Once you know your number, it stays reliable.
