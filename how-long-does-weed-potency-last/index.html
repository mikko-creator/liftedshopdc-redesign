Does Weed Lose Potency Over Time?

ORDER DELIVERY(771) 216-1684

Yes. Weed doesn’t spoil the way food does, but THC degrades steadily once the flower is harvested, converting into a compound called CBN that produces a duller, more sedating effect.

Research commonly cited in cannabis literature puts the loss at roughly 16% of THC content in the first year at room temperature, climbing to around 26% by year two and roughly 34% by year three.

Stored well, the flower stays good for six months to a year with little noticeable change. Stored badly, in a plastic bag on a sunny shelf, it can lose noticeable potency and most of its aroma in a matter of weeks.

Here’s what causes it and what to do about it.

How Much Potency Does Weed Lose?

Time at room temperatureApproximate THC loss
1 year~16%
2 years~27%
3 years~34%
4 years~41%

Those figures assume ordinary room conditions. Poor storage accelerates all of them considerably, and good storage slows them down.

The THC doesn’t vanish. It oxidizes into CBN, which is mildly psychoactive but much less so, and noticeably more sedating. That’s why old flower often feels less like a high and more like a heavy, sleepy fog. The total cannabinoid content hasn’t dropped much. The ratio has shifted.

Terpenes, the aromatic compounds responsible for smell and flavor, degrade faster than THC does. That’s why aroma is the first thing to go and the best early indicator that flower is on its way down.

The Four Things That Degrade Weed

Light: UV exposure is the single fastest way to break down THC. Direct sunlight will do more damage in a week than months in a dark cupboard. Clear glass jars on a windowsill are the classic mistake.

Air: Oxygen drives the conversion of THC into CBN. Every time you open a container, you replace the air with fresh oxygen, so a jar you dip into daily degrades faster than one you leave sealed.

Heat: Anything above roughly 70°F speeds up every other reaction and dries flower out. Keep it away from radiators, appliances, and anywhere that catches afternoon sun.

Humidity: This one cuts both ways. Too dry and the flower becomes brittle, the trichomes crumble off, and the smoke turns harsh. Too damp and you risk mould. The target range is 55% to 62% relative humidity, which is why two-way humidity packs are sold at 58% and 62%.

Does Weed Go Bad or Expire?

Not in the sense of becoming unusable. There’s no expiry date on cannabinoids, and properly stored flower from a year ago will still work.

What happens instead is gradual decline: less THC, more CBN, weaker aroma, harsher smoke.

The one genuine safety exception is mould. Flower stored above roughly 65% humidity, or sealed while still damp, can grow mould that isn’t always visible. That’s not a potency issue, it’s a health one, particularly for anyone with a compromised immune system or a respiratory condition. If flower smells musty, sour, or like a damp basement, or you can see any white or grey fuzz that isn’t trichomes, throw it out. It isn’t worth the risk.

How to Store Weed Properly

Use an airtight glass jar. Glass doesn’t react with terpenes the way plastic can, and a good seal keeps oxygen out. Small mason jars work perfectly well.

Choose dark glass, or keep the jar somewhere dark. UV-blocking jars are ideal. A clear jar in a closed cupboard achieves the same thing for free.

Add a two-way humidity pack. These hold the jar at a set humidity, releasing moisture when it’s dry and absorbing it when it’s damp. This is the single most effective upgrade for anyone storing flower more than a few weeks.

Match the jar to the amount. A gram rattling around in a large jar is mostly sitting in air. Use the smallest container the flower fits in.

Keep it cool and stable. Somewhere between 60 and 70°F, out of direct light, away from heat sources.

Skip the fridge and freezer. Both come up constantly, and neither is a good idea. Fridges cycle humidity, inviting condensation every time you take the jar out. Freezing makes trichomes brittle enough to snap off when handled, taking a good share of the potency with them.

Don’t vacuum seal flower. Vacuum sealing is often recommended, and it works well for many things, but the pressure crushes trichomes and compresses buds. Airtight is what you want, not airless.

Store ground flower separately and use it quickly. Grinding multiplies the surface area exposed to oxygen. Ground weed degrades far faster than whole buds, so grind what you’re about to use rather than stocking up.

How to Tell If Your Weed Has Degraded

Smell is the first signal. A vibrant aroma indicates the terpenes are intact. Faint, hay-like, or generically “green” means they’ve largely gone.

Texture. Properly stored flower has a slight give and a light spring. If it crumbles to dust at a touch, it’s overdried. If it feels damp or spongy, check carefully for mould.

Appearance. Trichomes should look frosty rather than brown. Browning is oxidation.

The smoke. Old flower burns harsher and hotter, because the terpenes that smoothed it out have gone.

Degraded flower is still perfectly usable, assuming no mould. Many people prefer slightly aged flower in the evening precisely because CBN makes it more sedating.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does weed lose potency over time?

Yes. THC oxidizes into CBN, a less psychoactive and more sedating compound. Commonly cited research suggests roughly 16% THC loss in the first year at room temperature, around 27% by year two, and about 34% by year three. Poor storage accelerates this considerably.

How much potency does weed lose in a year?

Roughly 16% of its THC content under ordinary room conditions. Kept in an airtight glass jar in a cool, dark place with humidity control, the loss is smaller. Left in a plastic bag in the light and warmth, it can be far greater.

Does weed go bad?

Not in the sense of spoiling. Cannabinoids have no expiry date, and old flower still works, just less strongly and with much less flavor. The exception is mould, which can grow in flower stored too damp and is a genuine health risk rather than a potency issue.

Does weed expire?

There’s no true expiry date. Potency and aroma decline gradually rather than reaching a cutoff. Stored well, flower is good for six months to a year with little noticeable change, and remains usable well beyond that.

How long does weed stay good?

Six months to a year in an airtight glass jar kept cool, dark, and at 55% to 62% humidity. In a plastic bag exposed to light and warmth, expect noticeable decline within weeks.

What’s the best way to store weed?

An airtight glass jar sized to the amount, kept in a cool, dark place with a two-way humidity pack holding it between 55% and 62%. Avoid plastic bags, clear jars in daylight, and heat sources.

Should I keep weed in the fridge or freezer?

No. Refrigerators cycle humidity, causing condensation each time the jar comes out. Freezing makes trichomes brittle, so they break off during handling, reducing potency. A cool cupboard is better than either.

Can old weed make you sick?

Not from age alone. Degraded flower is weaker, not harmful. Mould is the real concern, and it comes from moisture rather than time. If flower smells musty or sour, or shows white or grey fuzz, discard it.

Keep It Fresh, or Restock

Storage does most of the work here. An airtight glass jar, a humidity pack, and a dark cupboard will keep flower close to its original quality for the better part of a year.
