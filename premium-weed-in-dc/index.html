THC: SUPREME JOY

Scientists did not fully understand the active ingredient in marijuana until the 1960s when researchers observed that injecting THC into aggressive monkeys calmed and relaxed them. [3] This led the team to discover receptors in the brain that THC fits into perfectly, called cannabinoid receptors. In the 1990s, the same team found that our bodies produce compounds that fit into these receptors, which they named anandamides. These receptors, also known as endocannabinoid receptors, are found throughout the brain and were named not for THC consumption but for the Sanskrit word ananda – “supreme joy.”

GET THE MOST FROM YOUR HIGH

Medical marijuana and recreational marijuana are derived from the same plant, Cannabis sativa or Cannabis indica. Medical marijuana is used to alleviate symptoms and treat medical conditions. It typically contains higher levels of cannabidiol (CBD), which has therapeutic effects, and lower levels of tetrahydrocannabinol, the psychoactive compound that produces a “high.” Medical marijuana is typically prescribed by a physician and can only be obtained from licensed dispensaries or specific state programs regulating its distribution.

Recreational marijuana, on the other hand, is used for non-medical purposes and is consumed for its psychoactive effects. It typically contains higher levels of THC and lower levels of CBD than medical marijuana. Recreational marijuana is subject to legal regulations and can only be purchased in states where it is legal for adult use.

The legal regulations for medical and recreational marijuana differ significantly. Medical marijuana is legal in many states in the US, but it requires a prescription from a licensed physician and is subject to specific regulations regarding possession, use, and distribution. Recreational marijuana, on the other hand, is legal in fewer states and is subject to specific regulations regarding possession, use, and distribution for non-medical purposes.

WHAT ARE TERPENES?

Terpenes are organic compounds commonly found in plants, including cannabis. They are responsible for the distinct aromas and flavors of different strains of cannabis, as well as many other plants, such as lavender, pine, and citrus fruits.

In cannabis, terpenes are produced in the same glands as cannabinoids, such as THC and CBD. They can work synergistically with cannabinoids to produce specific effects, and some terpenes have been found to have therapeutic properties of their own. Many different types of terpenes are found in cannabis, and each strain can have a unique terpene profile. Some common terpenes found in cannabis include:

  • Myrcene
  • Limonene
  • Pinene
  • Caryophyllene

Myrcene, for example, is known for its sedative effects and is often found in strains used for relaxation and pain relief. In contrast, limonene is known for its uplifting effects and is often found in strains used for mood elevation.

Terpenes are becoming increasingly important in the cannabis industry, as more people are interested in different strains’ specific effects and flavors. Some companies are even beginning to isolate and extract specific terpenes for use in products such as oils, edibles, and topicals.
